# Glossary

Plain-language definitions for terms used in the Coach Intelligence Layer. One line each.

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| **1RM** | One-rep max — the maximum load you can lift for a single rep with good technique. |
| **e1RM** | Estimated 1RM — a 1RM calculated from a submaximal set using a formula (e.g. Epley, Brzycki); accuracy depends on reps and how far from failure the set was. |
| **RPE** | Rate of Perceived Exertion — a 1–10 scale of effort; RPE 10 = absolute maximum, RPE 8 = 2 reps left in the tank. |
| **RIR** | Reps in Reserve — how many reps you could still complete at the end of a set; RIR 2 = 2 reps left; the inverse of RPE proximity to failure. |
| **MV** | Maintenance Volume — the minimum number of weekly sets per muscle group to maintain current size and strength; typically 2–6 sets/week (heuristic). |
| **MEV** | Minimum Effective Volume — the minimum sets per week that produce noticeable adaptation; slightly above MV; individual and highly variable. |
| **MAV** | Maximum Adaptive Volume — the range of weekly sets where adaptation is maximized; the practical "sweet spot"; heuristic, not a measured value. |
| **MRV** | Maximum Recoverable Volume — the highest weekly set count you can recover from; exceeding it accumulates fatigue without additional adaptation; heuristic and individual. |
| **SFR** | Stimulus-to-Fatigue Ratio — a qualitative heuristic for how much hypertrophic stimulus an exercise provides per unit of systemic fatigue it generates; high SFR = efficient choice (e.g. pull-up vs. spinal deadlift). |
| **DUP** | Daily Undulating Periodization — varying rep ranges (e.g. strength / hypertrophy / endurance) across sessions within the same week rather than across mesocycles. |
| **AMRAP** | As Many Reps As Possible — performing a set to (or near) failure to gauge current capacity; used for testing and some max-rep progressions. |
| **Deload** | A planned reduction in training stress (volume, intensity, or both) to allow accumulated fatigue to dissipate and supercompensation to consolidate. |
| **Taper** | A deload-like reduction in volume (not intensity) in the final days before a competition or test; shorter duration than a deload week. |
| **ACWR** | Acute:Chronic Workload Ratio — the ratio of the current week's load to a rolling 4-week average; used as a load-spike indicator, not a validated injury risk score. |
| **DOMS** | Delayed Onset Muscle Soreness — muscle soreness peaking 24–72 h after a novel or high-eccentric training stimulus; a sign of tissue disruption, not a reliable proxy for hypertrophic stimulus. |
| **Autoregulation** | Adjusting training variables (load, reps, sets) on the fly based on readiness and performance signals rather than following a fixed prescription regardless of daily form. |
| **Double progression** | A rep-range-then-load scheme: stay at a given load until you hit the top of the rep range across all sets, then increase load; simple and effective for hypertrophy training. |
| **Axial loading** | Load applied along the spine's axis (e.g. back squat, deadlift, overhead press); accumulates compressive and shear forces on the lumbar spine; tracked separately in the exercise ontology. |
| **NSCA core vs. assistance** | The NSCA classification of exercises: *core* structural-power (Olympic lifts), *core structural* (multi-joint, high load — squat, deadlift, bench), *core* (major multi-joint), and *assistance* (single-joint, smaller muscles, prehab). |
